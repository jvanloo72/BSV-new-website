faqs:
  - question: "What's the difference between an inbound and an outbound technology license?"
    answer: "An inbound license is one where your company takes a license to use technology owned by someone else; an outbound license is one where your company grants others the right to use technology you own. Each raises different questions about scope, payment, and what happens if the relationship ends."
  - question: "When should a startup consider filing for patent protection?"
    answer: "Timing matters because, in the United States, public disclosure or sale of an invention can start a clock that limits the ability to file later. A startup generally benefits from talking with patent counsel before publicly disclosing or selling a new invention so the available options are understood early."
  - question: "What is open-source compliance and why does it matter in a deal?"
    answer: "Open-source software comes with license terms, and some of those terms place obligations on how the software and anything built with it can be used or distributed. In an acquisition or financing, a buyer or investor will often review how open-source software has been used, so understanding your obligations ahead of time helps avoid surprises in diligence."
  - question: "What does it mean that an attorney is registered to practice before the USPTO?"

BSV's intellectual-property and technology practice serves clients across
networking, data centers, medical devices, and eCommerce. We work on the full
arc of a technology company's IP needs — building and protecting a patent
portfolio, licensing technology in and out, navigating open-source obligations,
and handling the software and data agreements that underpin modern products.

Our lead attorney for this practice is registered to practice before the U.S.
Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) and brings a background in physics and
patent strategy to the work. When an IP question becomes a deal question — a
licensing matter inside an acquisition, a diligence flag on open-source use —
the same team carries it through.

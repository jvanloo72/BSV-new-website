## Mergers & Acquisitions

**How much does an M&A lawyer cost?**
We bill hourly and give you an estimate of total cost up front, so you are not
left guessing what a matter will run. Rates depend on the deal and are something
we discuss with you one-on-one rather than publish on the site.

**When in a deal should a company bring in M&A counsel?**
The earlier the better — ideally before a term sheet or letter of intent is
signed. Decisions made at the very start of a deal, such as how the transaction
is structured, are often the hardest and most costly to change later.

**What's the difference between an asset sale and a stock sale?**
In a stock sale the buyer purchases the company's shares and generally takes the
business with its existing liabilities; in an asset sale the buyer purchases
specific assets and can often leave certain liabilities behind. Which structure
makes sense depends on tax treatment, the contracts involved, and what each side
is trying to accomplish.

**How long does a typical middle-market M&A deal take?**
Timelines vary widely with the size and complexity of the transaction, but many
middle-market deals run a few months from signing a letter of intent to closing.
Diligence findings, financing, and any required regulatory clearances are the
factors that most often affect the schedule.

**Will I work with a partner or be handed off to a junior associate?**
At BSV the partner you meet is the partner who works on your deal. The team is
deliberately small and senior so that every matter gets partner-level attention.

## Intellectual Property & Technology Transactions

**What's the difference between an inbound and an outbound technology license?**
An inbound license is one where your company takes a license to use technology
owned by someone else; an outbound license is one where your company grants
others the right to use technology you own. Each raises different questions about
scope, payment, and what happens if the relationship ends.

**When should a startup consider filing for patent protection?**
Timing matters because, in the United States, public disclosure or sale of an
invention can start a clock that limits the ability to file later. A startup
generally benefits from talking with patent counsel before publicly disclosing
or selling a new invention so the available options are understood early.

**What is open-source compliance and why does it matter in a deal?**
Open-source software comes with license terms, and some of those terms place
obligations on how the software and anything built with it can be used or
distributed. In an acquisition or financing, a buyer or investor will often
review how open-source software has been used, so understanding your obligations
ahead of time helps avoid surprises in diligence.

**What does it mean that an attorney is registered to practice before the USPTO?**
Registration to practice before the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) is
a factual credential that allows an attorney to represent clients in patent
matters before that office. It reflects that the attorney has met the USPTO's
requirements; it is not a claim of certification in any field.

## Tax

**What is a tax-free reorganization?**
A tax-free reorganization is a way of structuring a merger or acquisition so that
some or all of the gain that would otherwise be taxed at closing is deferred,
typically when sellers receive stock rather than cash. Whether a deal can qualify
depends on detailed rules, which is why the tax structure is best examined early.

**How is cryptocurrency taxed in an M&A transaction?**
When cryptocurrency is used as part of the consideration in a deal, its tax
treatment can differ from cash or stock and depends on the facts of the
transaction. Because the rules in this area are evolving, it helps to bring tax
counsel in early to understand how a particular structure would be treated.

**What tax issues arise when using cryptocurrency as compensation?**
Paying someone in cryptocurrency generally raises both income and employment-tax
questions, and the value used for tax purposes is typically measured at the time
the crypto is received. The specifics depend on who is being paid and how the
arrangement is structured.

**Do I owe U.S. tax if I expatriate?**
Giving up U.S. citizenship or long-term residency can trigger special tax rules,
including a potential exit tax for certain individuals. Whether and how those
rules apply depends on personal circumstances, so this is an area to review with
tax counsel before taking any step.
